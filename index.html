Day 0

Going to Skövde

Written by: Milan Scharmin

The day started quite early, I woke up at 7 o'clock and got ready to go the trainstation in Ostend. From there, I took 4 trains all the way to Schiphol Airport in The Netherlands. It was quite an annoying journey, because on the way there were railworks so I had to take a different train than originaly planned.

The flight to Stockholm was really good, I noticed that the plane had Wifi on-board which was quite nice. The journey on the plane was only around 1 hour and 40 mins so it wasn't even that bad. Time really flew by (Pun intended)!

Once I arrived in Stockholm, there were 2 more trains waiting for me. Both of them were High Speed Trains and you could really feel your ears popping.

I looked out the window almost the entire time. It was so fun to see the typical Swedish landscape and architecture. I managed to snap a picture of this beautiful Katrineholm train station.

The last train I took from Stockholm was around 2 hours but you barely noticed it.

By the time I arrived in Skövde, the sun was already settings. I checked into my hotel (Quality Prisma Hotel) and we decided to go out and explore Skövde.

Some students from Howest had already arrived and asked other students and Nico to come join them in at a local bar.

So on the way there my friend and I noticed this beautiful building. Apparently, it's a hotel called Scandic Billingen hotel.

It was nice meeting new people here, some people I had seen in class but never talked to before. The bar we stayed at is called Rådhuscaféet.

Our first impressions were a bit mixed, we really didn't see any people outside. Most of the bars close at around 11 o'clock or sometimes a bit later. It's so different from what we're used to in Belgium. Because of this, we decided to leave and go to the Hostel where a lot of students stayed at and chatted for an hour or so.

Then it was time to head back to our hotel and get some rest.

Day 3

Grinding CTF & Military Party

Written by: Noah Chang

We started our day off socializing with the Swedish students and the teachers. By now we already know who they are, but we decided we wanted to know them better. Here is a picture with our teacher and friend Johan; on this day we got to know him better. One of the things we remember from this interaction is that he told us his 2 daughters are our age ;).

We also worked on this CTF this day. The challenge that stood out most for me is the healthpotion challenge. You had to do the command man healthpotion and then got an OVA file. This file can be put into VMware and boot up a Debian Linux machine. Then on that machine, there were a lot of pieces to find and when you puzzle them together you get the flag. An amazing CTF challenge.

After school we were all hungry. We all stayed in different accommodations so we split up. I went back to my Airbnb and cooked some pasta for me and my roommate.

In the evening there was a bar event the Swedish students organized so we assembled again with the one and only, legendary, Nico to go to the bar.

After the student bar we went to the city to meet new people. There we ran into a platoon of military people that were friendly (at first). We made new friends and had a lot of friendly & funny interactions with them.

After a long day of partying we all went our separate ways again to go rest for the next day. Namaste my dudes.

Day 4

Will we win the CTF? Fight for the first place!

Written by: Karolina Supruniuk

We woke up excited, anticipating the last day of the CTF competition. During this week, we were experiencing ups and downs between solving the challenges and being stuck on some of them for days. Today promised new challenges and resolution to all the effort we put in the previous ones. But first, my roommate and I take the usual 1 hour walk to the campus through the beautiful countryside.

Arriving just as the new challenges were released, with only fifteen minutes left before submissions opened, me and my teammates united to prepare our answers to the riddles. The goal is clear - submit the flags as fast as possible, since the team who solves them first receives bonus points. Further in the blog, I will explain how much it impacted the chances of our victory!

In many challenges we submitted first, but the majority of them have already been solved when we got our answers right. It didn't discourage us, and we focused on solving the other tasks we couldn't get from the past few days. But there is one challenge that everyone stopped looking at - The Marcus Theorem. Rumors say it's unsolvable.

During the last hour of the challenge, everyone was exhausted. All the challenges that could be solved were solved, while some of the old ones were still left with 0 solves. People gathered around our table to discuss the experience. Gossiping about who will win - the "apt install braincell" - my team, or "Godslayers" - the team of Xander. With just 10 points, we were leading the game, but soon it's about to change, because Godslayers have the first solve on 20 challenges more than us!

With that conclusion, the school day was over, and it was time to head to the Skövde Cultural Centre. Right after entering the museum part of the Cultural Centre, we were greeted with this precious exhibit.

The more we explored the museum, the more we got confused. As avant-garde as this museum is, we had a hard time determining if the objects are leftovers from renovating work or if they are the next exhibits.

On the second floor of the museum, we entered the final room, where the paintings of Christine Waller caught everyone's eye. The unique technique of combining digitally taken pictures and oil paint makes them mesmerizing.

After the long day of new experiences, joys and struggles, we all were exhausted. It's time we part ways and rest, since tomorrow is the day the winner is announced and a long journey home begins. On the walk back, we saw this interesting sign. Swedish people care about animals!

Day 5

The Final Day

Written by: Kjani Van Eenaeme

The last morning in Skövde had a different feeling to it. No more CTF challenges to grind, no more late nights — just the anticipation of finding out who actually won.

We gathered one last time at the university, and the results were finally revealed. Godslayers took first place with 39,140 points, narrowly beating apt install braincell who came in second with 37,150. A gap of less than 2,000 points — it really could have gone either way. Our team, Imposters, landed a respectable 4th place with 34,350 points. Not bad at all considering the competition.

The CTF creators took the floor for a final word — going over some of the challenges and the intended solutions. The highlight was finally getting the truth about the Marcus Theorem: it was never actually solvable. The creator just took a random string, hashed it a few times, and called it a challenge. Zero solves, zero shame — nobody stood a chance.

And then, just like that, it was over. We took the group photo in front of Högskolan i Skövde, said our goodbyes to Johan and the Swedish students, and slowly started making our way home over the next day or two.

Looking back, this week was more than just a CTF. It was a first solo trip abroad, new friendships, military encounters, pasta cooked in an Airbnb, and bars that close at 11. Sweden was nothing like Belgium — and that was exactly the point.
